## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- sk – skip
- sp – space
- st – stitch

**Special Stitches**:
- **Lace Shell**: (5 dc in the same stitch)

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size**: 
  - Length: 35.5 cm (14 in)
  - Width: 2.5 cm (1 in)
  - Ties: 114 cm (45 in) each (can be adjusted based on personal preference)

- **Gauge**: 10 dc and 6 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in width. To check gauge, crochet a swatch of 20 dc x 12 rows. If your swatch does not match the gauge, adjust your yarn or hook size accordingly.

## How to Crochet a Choker (Step by Step)

### Choker Base
The choker base is crocheted flat, starting with a chain.

1. Chain 50. 
   - (50 ch)
   
2. Row 1: In the 4th ch from hook (counts as first dc), dc in each ch across.
   - (48 dc)

3. Row 2: Ch 3, turn. **Lace Shell** in the first st, *sk next 2 sts, Lace Shell in the next st*, repeat from *to* until the end, sk last st, dc in the last st.
   - (16 Lace Shells, 2 dc in last st)

4. Row 3: Repeat Row 2.
   - (16 Lace Shells, 2 dc in last st)

5. Row 4: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each dc and lace shell across.
   - (50 sc)

6. Row 5: Ch 1, turn. Sc in next 5 sts, ch 45 (for ties), sk 25 sts, sc in next 18 sts.
   - (50 sts)

7. Row 6: Ch 1, turn. Sc in all sc across.
   - (50 sc)

### Ties
The ties are simple chains attached to the choker for an adjustable fit.

8. Tie off and cut yarn after last sc. Weave in all ends.

### Assembly
The assembly consists of making sure the ties are secure.

- If a button is used, sew it to the end of a tie. 
- The other tie will wrap around and fasten to the button or can be tied in a bow for an adjustable fit.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Choker
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your lace shells.
- Count your stitches as you work, especially in lace sections to maintain spacing.
- Try on your choker as you go to ensure it's the right length for you.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My lace shells are uneven." – This may happen if you skip different numbers of stitches. Always count carefully!
- "The choker feels too tight." – Make sure to leave a comfortable length for the ties. You can always add more chains.
- "I'm not getting the right gauge." – Check your yarn and hook size; adjusting them may help match the gauge.

## How to Care for Your Choker
Hand wash in cold water and lay flat to dry to maintain the shape and integrity of the lace pattern.
