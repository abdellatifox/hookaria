This elegant midnight star crochet shawl features a beautiful triangular shape and a captivating star motif. The combination of rich deep navy and silver yarn gives it a striking look, perfect for evening outings or as a cozy wrap on cooler nights. The openwork stitch pattern adds a delicate yet stylish element to your wardrobe.

This pattern is suitable for intermediate crocheters looking to enhance their skills with openwork techniques and shaping. You'll enjoy creating this shawl while exploring unique stitches and increasing your confidence with larger projects.

- Versatile styling – wrap it around your shoulders or drape it as a scarf.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
No, this pattern requires some experience with basic crochet stitches and openwork techniques. If you've mastered single crochet and double crochet stitches, you're ready to dive into this shawl!

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- dc – double crochet
- sp – space
- inc – increase
- tr – treble crochet
- * – repeat
- BLO – back loop only

## Size and Gauge
- Finished Size: 140 × 70 cm (55 × 28 in)
- Gauge: 14 dc and 7 rows = 10 cm.

To check your gauge, measure how many double crochets and rows fit in a 10 cm square. If your swatch is too small, try a larger hook. If it’s too big, use a smaller hook.

## The Pattern

### Step 1: Create the Foundation Chain
The shawl begins with a foundation chain that forms the top edge.
Ch 120, then sl st to the first ch to join, forming a ring. (120 ch)

### Step 2: Crocheting the Shawl
We'll work in rows to create the triangular shape.

Row 1: Ch 3 (counts as first dc), *dc in next ch, repeat from * across, ending with dc in the last ch. Turn. (120 dc)

Row 2: Ch 4 (counts as first tr), *tr in next dc, ch 1, skip next dc; repeat from * across, ending with tr in the last dc. Turn. (60 trs, 59 ch-1 spaces)

Row 3: Ch 3 (counts as first dc), *dc in next tr, dc in ch-1 sp; repeat from * across, ending with dc in the last tr. Turn. (120 dc)

Row 4: Ch 1, sc in first dc, *ch 4, skip next dc, sc in next dc; repeat from * across, ending with sc in the last dc. Turn. (60 sc)

Row 5: Ch 1, turn; *BLO hdc in each of the next 60 sc. Finish off. (60 hdc)

### Step 3: Creating the Star Motif
We'll now add the beautiful star motif to the shawl.

Round 1: Join silver yarn in any ch-4 sp from Row 4. Ch 3 (counts as first dc), *dc in same sp, ch 1, 2 dc; repeat from * around. Join with sl st to top of beginning ch-3.

### Finishing
Once you've finished the shawl, fasten off the yarn and weave in the ends using a tapestry needle. Block the shawl gently to open up the stitches and shape it into a graceful triangle.

## Finishing Tip: Secure Your Ends
Be sure to weave in all ends securely so your shawl stays neat and tidy. This will also prevent any unraveling over time.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Midnight Star Shawl
- Use stitch markers to mark the beginning of each row.
- Count your stitches regularly to ensure you’re on track with your pattern.
- Maintain even tension as you crochet to achieve a consistent appearance.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
"My shawl is too small." – You may need to check your gauge. Try using a larger hook for a bigger shawl.
"The star motifs look uneven." – This can happen if your tension varies. Practice consistency with your stitches.
"My shawl has holes where it shouldn't." – Review the instructions for skipped stitches; ensure you follow them closely.

## How to Care for Your Midnight Star Shawl
Hand wash your shawl in cold water with a gentle detergent. Lay it flat to dry to maintain its shape and intricate openwork design.

## FAQ
### How do I resize this shawl?
To resize the shawl, simply adjust the number of chains in your foundation chain and ensure your rows maintain symmetry concerning the original instructions.

### Can I use a different yarn weight?
Yes, just be sure to adjust your hook size and check your gauge. A heavier yarn may make a warm, cozy wrap, while a lighter yarn gives a more delicate look.

### How long will this project take?
This project typically takes around 8–12 hours to complete, depending on your skill level and pace.
