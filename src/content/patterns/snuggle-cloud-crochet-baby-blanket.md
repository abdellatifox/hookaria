This snuggle cloud crochet baby blanket is perfect for your little one's cozy naps. Crafted to resemble soft, billowy clouds, this project features a unique puff stitch pattern and a delicate picot trim around the edges. You'll enjoy making this blanket using basic stitches and techniques that are easy to follow.

This pattern is suitable for beginners and those looking to practice their skills with stitches like the puff stitch and picot trim. With just a little patience and practice, you'll be creating beautiful blankets for gifts or your own baby nursery.

## Why You'll Love This Baby Blanket
- Soft and cozy – using airy yarn makes it perfect for naptime.
- Cloud-like texture – the puff stitch creates a plush feel.
- Versatile colors – blends well with any nursery decor.
- Easy to follow – ideal for beginners and those wanting to expand their skills.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern is a great introduction to crochet. You will learn the puff stitch and picot stitch, both of which are not too difficult and will help improve your crochet skills.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- puff – puff stitch: yarn over, insert hook into stitch, yarn over, pull through, yarn over, pull through two loops on hook, repeat twice more, yarn over, pull through all loops.
- picot – picot: ch 3, sl st in the first ch.

## Size and Gauge
- Finished size: 76 × 91 cm (30 × 36 in)
- Gauge: 12 puff stitches and 10 rows = 10 cm (4 in).
To check your gauge, work a small swatch in the pattern before starting the blanket. 

## Step 1: Create the Base
The blanket is made in rows, starting with a foundation chain.

Row 1: Ch 105. Work 1 sc in the second ch from the hook. Work 1 sc in each ch across. (104 sc)

Row 2: Ch 1 (does not count as a stitch), turn. Work 1 sc in each stitch across. (104 sc)

## Step 2: Puff Stitch Rows
We'll create the cloud-like texture with puff stitches.

Row 3: Ch 1, turn. *Puff in next stitch, skip next stitch, repeat from * across. Finish with 1 sc in the last stitch. (52 puff stitches, 1 sc)

Row 4: Ch 1, turn. Work 1 sc in each puff across. (52 sc)

## Step 3: Repeat Puff Stitch Pattern
Continue to repeat Rows 3 and 4 until the blanket reaches a total length of 76 cm (30 in).

## Step 4: Adding the Picot Trim
Let's finish the blanket with a charming picot trim.

Row 1: Ch 1, turn. Work 1 sc in each stitch across. (104 sc)

Row 2: Ch 1, turn. *Ch 3, sl st in the first ch (picot), sc in the next stitch, repeat from * across. Finish with a sl st in the last stitch.

## How to Join Your Granny Squares
To join the blanket, fasten off and weave in all ends securely using a tapestry needle.

## Finishing Tip: Secure Your Ends
When finishing your blanket, make sure to weave in all loose ends with a tapestry needle. This will help keep your blanket looking tidy.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Baby Blanket
- Use stitch markers to help keep track of rows and puff stitches.
- Count your stitches regularly to avoid mistakes.
- Maintain consistent tension for an even look.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- My blanket is too small. I must not have counted my foundation chains correctly. Make sure to count them while chaining to ensure you have the right number.
- The stitches seem too tight. I may be pulling the yarn too hard. Try to maintain an even tension throughout your work.
- My blanket curls at the edges. This can happen if the stitches are too tight. Experiment with a larger hook size for better results.

## How to Care for Your Baby Blanket
To care for your blanket, hand wash in cold water and lay flat to dry. Avoid wringing or twisting the yarn to maintain its fluffy structure.

Yes! You can substitute any soft, worsted weight yarn that practices similar drape and texture.
