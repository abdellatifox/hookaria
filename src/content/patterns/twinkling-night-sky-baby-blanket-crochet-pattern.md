This enchanting Twinkling Night Sky Baby Blanket features a soft, textured design reminiscent of a star-filled sky. The blanket is crafted with deep navy blue and bright yellow yarn, creating a beautiful contrast that mimics a tranquil night. You’ll be working with a puff stitch technique to create playful stars, which may sound challenging but becomes easier with practice. This pattern is perfect for those looking to enhance their skills with a lovely project that will keep your little one cozy.

## Why You'll Love This Baby Blanket
- Soft and snuggly – perfect for keeping your baby warm and comfortable.
- Unique texture – the puff stitch adds depth, resembling twinkling stars in the night sky.
- Easy to customize – simply change the colors to make it your own.
- Fun to make – enjoy working with basic stitches and a fun technique for a beautiful finish.

## Skill Level: Easy
You should know the following stitches: 
- Ch – chain
- Sc – single crochet
- Puff stitch – a stitch that creates a raised texture, which we'll explain in the abbreviations section.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- Ch – chain
- Sl st – slip stitch
- Sc – single crochet
- Puff st – puff stitch: yarn over, insert your hook into the designated stitch, yarn over again, pull through, yarn over again, and pull through the first two loops on your hook. Repeat this sequence one more time in the same stitch, then yarn over and pull through all three loops.
- Sp – space
- Inc – increase

## Size and Gauge
- Finished size: 76 × 102 cm (30 × 40 in)
- Gauge: 14 dc and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in)
To measure gauge, crochet a small swatch and count the stitches. If your blanket is larger or smaller than intended, adjust your hook size as needed.

## The Pattern
The blanket is crocheted in rows. 

### Row 1: 
Ch 100. Work 1 sc in the 2nd ch from the hook, then 1 sc in each ch across. Turn. (99 sc) 

### Row 2: 
Ch 1 (this does not count as a stitch), work 1 sc in the first sc, then *ch 1, skip the next sc, 1 sc in the next sc*; repeat from * across. You’ll end with a sc in the last stitch. Turn.

### Row 3: 
Ch 1, work 1 sc in the first space, *ch 1, skip the next sc, 1 sc in the next space*; repeat from * across. Turn. 

### Row 4: 
Ch 1, work 1 sc in the first sc. Puff st in the next sp: yarn over, insert hook, yarn over and pull through, yarn over and pull through the first two loops, yarn over and pull through the remaining loops. *1 sc in the next sc, puff st in the next sp*; repeat from * until the last stitch. End with 1 sc in the last stitch. Turn.

### Row 5: 
Ch 1, work 1 sc in the first sc, *ch 1, skip the puff st, 1 sc in the next sc*; repeat from * across. Turn. 

### Row 6: 
Ch 1, work 1 sc in the first space, *ch 1, skip the next sc, 1 sc in the next space*; repeat from * across. Turn.

### Row 7: 
Ch 1, work 1 sc in the first sc, puff st in the next sp; repeat from * across. End with 1 sc in the last stitch. Turn.

Repeat Rows 5–7 until the blanket reaches 76 cm (30 in) or your desired length.

## Finishing Tip: Secure Your Ends
Once you reach the final desired height, fasten off and weave in all ends using a tapestry needle. This will help to give your blanket a clean look.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Baby Blanket 
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rows or stitches.
- Count your stitches regularly to avoid running out of yarn.
- Maintain an even tension for a uniform look.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- My stars look uneven. – This can happen if the puff stitches are not pulled up consistently. Practice to create a more uniform appearance.
- The edges curl up. – This might be due to too many stitches or uneven tension. Ensure you’re following the pattern closely.
- My rows don’t seem straight. – If your rows are leaning, try counting your starting chains to ensure they match your gauge.

## How to Care for Your Baby Blanket
To keep your blanket soft and clean, hand wash it in cold water with a gentle detergent. Lay it flat to dry and avoid direct sunlight to maintain the colors.

### What yarn can I use instead of the recommended one?
You can use any worsted weight yarn. Just be sure to check the gauge for the best results.
