This cozy hooded baby jacket is designed to keep little ones warm while looking adorable. Featuring a bright mustard yellow color, this jacket is perfect for chilly days. It uses simple stitches like double crochet and single crochet, making it both enjoyable and beginner-friendly.

This pattern is suitable for new crocheters who want to create a practical piece for babies aged 0-12 months. You’ll learn how to piece together a jacket, including working a hood and attaching sleeves.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern is perfect for beginners. It focuses on basic stitches like double crochet and single crochet, so you can feel confident as you stitch along. Plus, each step is broken down clearly to guide you through the making process.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- sc – single crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- inc – increase (2 stitches in the same stitch)

## Gauge and Finished Size
- Finished Measurements: 
  - 0-3 months: Chest: 50.8 cm (20 in), Length: 25.4 cm (10 in), Sleeve: 17.8 cm (7 in)
  - 3-6 months: Chest: 55.9 cm (22 in), Length: 27.9 cm (11 in), Sleeve: 20.3 cm (8 in)
  - 6-12 months: Chest: 61 cm (24 in), Length: 30.5 cm (12 in), Sleeve: 22.9 cm (9 in)
- Gauge: 12 dc and 15 rows = 10 cm (4 in)

To measure your gauge, make a swatch of 20 stitches in double crochet for 10 cm (4 in). If your gauge doesn’t match, adjust your hook size accordingly.

## How to Crochet the Mustard Yellow Hooded Baby Jacket (Step by Step)

### Jacket Body
The body of the jacket is worked flat starting with a foundation chain.  
1. **Foundation Chain**: Ch 74 (82 for 3-6 months, ch 90 for 6-12 months).  
2. **Row 1**: Dc in the 4th ch from the hook, dc in each ch across (72, 80, 88 dc).  
3. **Row 2–12**: Ch 3, turn. Dc in each st across (72, 80, 88 dc).  
4. **Row 13**: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across (72, 80, 88 sc).  
5. **Row 14**: Ch 3, turn. Dc in each st across (72, 80, 88 dc).  
6. **Row 15**: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across (72, 80, 88 sc).  
7. **Row 16**: Ch 1, turn. Sc for 12 (14, 16) st, inc, sc for 24 (28, 32) st, inc, sc for 12 (14, 16) st, inc (76, 84, 92 sc).

### Hood
The hood is made separately and then attached to the body.  
8. **Hood Foundation**: Ch 26 (28, 30).  
9. **Rows 1–4**: Dc in each ch across (24, 26, 28 dc).  
10. **Row 5**: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across.  
11. **Row 6**: Ch 3, turn. Dc in each st across.

### Assembly
Now let’s assemble the jacket!  
12. **Joining Body & Hood**: Align the hood along the neckline of the body, pinning it in place. With right sides together, slip stitch the edges of the hood to the body neckline.  
13. **Sleeves**: Attach yarn to each armhole, ch 1 and work (20, 24, 28) sc around each armhole. Use stitch markers to mark the beginning of each round.  
14. **Casting Off Sleeves**: Continue for 6 rounds for 0-3 months, 8 rounds for 3-6 months, and 10 rounds for 6-12 months. Finish off.

### Finishing Touches
It's almost done!  
15. Attach a button at the waist using the yarn needle.  
16. Weave in all ends and block the jacket.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Baby Jacket
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rows and rounds.
- Count your stitches at the end of each row to avoid any unevenness.
- Maintain consistent tension throughout your project for a neat finish.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My stitches are too loose." – This usually means your tension is too relaxed. Try holding the yarn a bit tighter.
- "The jacket is too small." – Make sure to check your gauge before starting. Adjust your hook size if necessary.
- "My project is uneven." – This could happen if you lose count of your stitches. Use a stitch marker and count as you go!

## How to Care for Your Hooded Baby Jacket
To keep your jacket looking its best, hand wash it in cool water and lay it flat to dry. Avoid wringing out the fabric and never iron it.

### FAQ
### How many yards of yarn do I need for each size?
- For the 0-3 months size, you'll need about 300 yards. The 3-6 months size requires around 350 yards, and for 6-12 months, about 400 yards.

### Can I use a different yarn weight?
- Yes, you can use a different yarn weight, but keep in mind that this will change the finished size and drape of the jacket. Adjust your gauge accordingly.

### How do I resize this pattern?
- To resize, start with a longer or shorter foundation chain. Just ensure the number of stitches matched the rows as indicated in each step.
