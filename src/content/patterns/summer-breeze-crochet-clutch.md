## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern uses simple stitches like single crochet and double crochet. You'll learn the shell stitch, which is fun and easy to master. 

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain  
- sl st – slip stitch  
- sc – single crochet  
- dc – double crochet  
- hdc – half double crochet  
- sp – space

## How to Make a Shell Stitch
1. Yarn over and insert your hook into the designated stitch or space.  
2. Yarn over again and pull through to have three loops on your hook.  
3. Yarn over and pull through the first two loops on your hook.  
4. Yarn over again and pull through the last two loops. You’ve now completed a double crochet!

## Size and Gauge
- Finished Size: 25 × 15 cm (10 × 6 in) when folded.  
- Gauge: 14 dc and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in).  
To measure gauge, make a small swatch of the shell stitch. Adjust your hook size if necessary to match gauge.

## Step 1: Create the Base 
The base of the clutch is worked in rows.  
Row 1: Ch 34. (This is your foundation chain.)  
Row 2: Sc in the second ch from the hook and in each ch across. Turn. (33 sc)  
Row 3: Ch 3 (this counts as your first dc), dc in the first sc. *Skip the next sc, make 5 dc in the next sc (this forms the shell stitch). Skip the next sc. Repeat from * across, ending with a dc in the last sc. Turn. (6 shells)  
Row 4: Ch 1 (does not count as a stitch), sc in each dc across. Turn. (6 sc)  
Rows 5–12: Repeat Rows 3 and 4 until the piece measures approximately 15 cm (6 in) from the base.

## Step 2: Create the Fold-Over Flap 
The flap is made from the same base using shell stitches.  
Row 13: Ch 1, sc in the first sc, dc in the next sc, *skip the next sc, five dc in the next sc, skip the next sc. Repeat from * across. Turn.  
Row 14: Ch 1 (does not count as a stitch), sc in each dc across. Turn.  
Rows 15–18: Repeat Rows 13 and 14 to form the desired length of the flap, measuring approximately 10 cm (4 in) from Row 12.

## How to Finish the Clutch
1. Ch 1 and sc around the entire edge of the clutch to finish. This adds structure.  
2. Fasten off and weave in ends.  

## Beginner Tips
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rows.  
- Count your stitches regularly to ensure evenness.  
- Keep your tension consistent for a neat finish.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
"My shell stitches look uneven." – This may be due to inconsistent tension. Try to keep your yarn tight but not too tight.  
"My flap is too short." – If this happens, add a few more rows of shell stitch to reach the desired length.  
"The clasp won't stay closed." – Ensure that you stitch the clasp securely to the flap. You might add extra stitches around the clasp for more support.

## How to Care for Your Clutch
To keep your clutch looking its best, hand wash it gently in cold water. Lay it flat to dry out of direct sunlight, which can help preserve the colors.

## FAQ
### How many stitches do I need for a clutch?
You will need 34 chains for the foundation when creating this clutch.

### Can I use a different type of yarn?
Yes! You can substitute with any soft yarn suitable for the same weight. Just remember to check your gauge.

### How long will it take to make this clutch?
It should take you about 4–6 hours to complete, depending on your crochet speed.
