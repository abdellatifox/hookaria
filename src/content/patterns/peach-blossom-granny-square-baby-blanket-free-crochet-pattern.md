This adorable Peach Blossom Granny Square Baby Blanket features soft, pastel colors and delicate granny squares perfect for a baby's nursery. It's a simple yet beautiful design suitable for beginners to crocheters. You’ll learn how to make charming granny squares and join them to create a lovely blanket.

This pattern is designed for anyone ready to create something special for a little one. With straightforward steps and helpful tips, you’ll find joy in the process and the finished project.

## Why You'll Love This Baby Blanket
- Adorable design – perfect for a nursery or as a gift.
- Simple construction – great for beginners to practice their skills.
- Versatile colors – customize with your favorite pastel shades.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern only requires basic stitches like chain, slip stitch, and double crochet. Each step is broken down clearly, making it easy to follow along. You'll gain confidence while creating a beautiful piece.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- dc – double crochet
- sp – space
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- sc2tog – single crochet two together (decrease)

### Special Stitches
- Granny Square – A square made using clusters of double crochets in designated spaces, creating a lacy effect.

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size**: Approximately 91 × 91 cm (36 × 36 in)
- **Gauge**: 5 granny squares (15 × 15 cm each) should measure approximately 30 × 30 cm (12 × 12 in). To check gauge, make a sample granny square. If your squares are smaller, try a larger hook; if they are larger, try a smaller hook.

## The Pattern

### Part A: Granny Squares (Make 9)
The granny squares are crocheted in rounds, and each square can feature different colors for a fun effect. 

1. **Square 1 (Peach)**: 
   - Start with Color A (Peach).
   - **Rnd 1**: Ch 4, join with sl st to form a ring. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), then 2 dc in ring, ch 2, *3 dc in ring, ch 2* repeat from * to * two more times. Join to top of ch 3 with sl st. (12 dc, 4 ch-2 sp)
   
2. **Rnd 2**: Sl st to first ch-2 sp. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in same sp, ch 2, *3 dc in next ch-2 sp, ch 2* repeat around. Join to top of ch 3 with sl st. (24 dc, 4 ch-2 sp)

3. **Rnd 3**: Sl st to first ch-2 sp. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in same sp, ch 1, *3 dc in next ch-2 sp, ch 1* repeat around. Join to top of ch 3 with sl st. (36 dc, 4 ch-1 sp)

4. **Rnd 4**: Sl st to first ch-1 sp. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in same sp, ch 2, *3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 2* repeat around. Join to top of ch 3 with sl st. (48 dc, 4 ch-2 sp)
  
5. Fasten off. Weave in ends.

6. **Repeat Rounds 1–4** for 8 more squares, alternating colors as follows:
   - Square 2: Cream (Color B)
   - Square 3: Peach (Color A)
   - Square 4: Cream (Color B)
   - Square 5: Pale Green (Color C)
   - Square 6: Peach (Color A)
   - Square 7: Cream (Color B)
   - Square 8: Pale Green (Color C)
   - Square 9: Peach (Color A)

### Part B: Joining Squares
The squares are arranged and joined together to form the blanket.

1. Arrange squares in a 3 × 3 layout. Join squares using Color B (Cream).
2. Use the whip stitch or slip stitch method to join the squares together edge to edge.

### Part C: Border
The border adds a nice finishing touch to your blanket.

1. **Border Round**: With Color B (Cream), attach yarn to any corner of the blanket.
2. Ch 1, sc into same space, *sc in each st to corner, 3 sc in corner* repeat around ending with a slip stitch to the first sc.
3. Fasten off and weave in all ends.

## Beginner Tips
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rounds.
- Count your stitches as you go to ensure a consistent look.
- Pay attention to tension; it should be consistent for an even blanket.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My squares are uneven." – This might be due to inconsistent tension; focus on keeping your tension even as you work.
- "The edges are curling up." – This can happen if your stitches are too tight; try loosening your tension.
- "The joins look messy." – Take your time with the joining; ensure you pull your yarn snug but not too tightly.

## How to Care for Your Baby Blanket
Machine wash in cold water, tumble dry on low, and avoid bleach to keep your blanket soft and looking new.

## FAQ
### How many granny squares do I need for a blanket?
For this blanket, you need 9 granny squares arranged in a 3 × 3 layout.

### Can I use different colors?
Absolutely! Feel free to mix in other pastel colors or shades to match your nursery.

### What if my gauge is off?
If your squares are a different size, try adjusting your hook size. A larger hook will make bigger squares, while a smaller hook will make smaller squares.
