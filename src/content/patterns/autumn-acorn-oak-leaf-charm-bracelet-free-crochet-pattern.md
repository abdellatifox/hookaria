This autumn acorn oak leaf charm bracelet is a delightful way to celebrate the beauty of the fall season. This charming accessory features acorn and oak leaf motifs and is perfect for casual wear or as a statement piece. You'll be employing basic stitches and techniques like the magic ring, making this project enjoyable and satisfying.

This pattern is suitable for those with a little crochet experience or adventurous beginners who are ready to try something new. You'll learn how to create and assemble charming motifs while crafting a lovely accessory that you can wear or gift.

## Skill Level: Easy
This project uses basic crochet stitches, including single crochet, chain, and slip stitch. The new technique you will learn is the magic ring, which is used to start the motifs.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sc – single crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- dec – decrease
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- rnd – round
- inc – increase

**Magic Ring** – Used to start the acorn and leaf motifs to allow for a tight center: Wrap yarn around fingers, insert hook into the loop, pull up a loop, then work stitches into the ring.

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished bracelet length:** Approximately 7.5 inches (19 cm)
- **Gauge:** 16 sc and 18 rows = 10 cm (4 inches).
To check gauge, make a small swatch of 16 sc x 18 rows. Adjust your hook size as needed to match the gauge.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### 1. Acorn Motif (Make 2)
The acorn motif is made in rounds using chestnut brown yarn.
1. Create a magic ring, ch 1.
2. Round 1: Work 6 sc into the ring. *(6 sts)*
3. Round 2: Inc in each st around. *(12 sts)*
4. Round 3: *1 sc, inc* around. *(18 sts)*
5. Round 4: Work 1 sc in each st around. *(18 sts)*
6. Round 5: Using pumpkin orange, switch yarn; work 1 sc in each st around. *(18 sts)*
7. Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing.

### 2. Oak Leaf Motif (Make 2)
The leaf motif is worked in rows with olive green yarn.
1. Ch 7.
2. Row 1: Starting in 2nd ch from hook, sc in next 5 ch; 3 sc in last ch; 1 sc in next 5 ch. Turn. *(12 sts)*
3. Row 2: Ch 1, sc in first 5 sts, inc in next st, sc in next 5 sts. Turn. *(15 sts)*
4. Row 3: Ch 1, sc in first 6 sts, inc, sc in next 7 sts. Turn. *(16 sts)*
5. Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing.

### 3. Assembly
Now, we will assemble the bracelet.
1. **Attach Acorns:** Use the tapestry needle and long ends from the acorn motifs to sew them near the center of the bracelet.
2. **Attach Leaf Motifs:** Sew each leaf on either side of the acorns using the remaining long ends from the leaf motifs.
3. **Secure with Glue:** Optionally, add a bit of craft glue to the base of the acorns for extra stability.
4. **Make the Strap:** Chain 60 (or desired length to fit your wrist) with olive green, join with a sl st to form a loop.
5. **Finish the Strap:** Ch 1, sc in each st around for 2 rounds, then fasten off.

### 4. Attachments
1. Use pliers to open each jump ring, attaching one to the end of the bracelet. Secure the lobster clasp to the other end.

## Finishing
Weave in all loose ends using the tapestry needle. If you'd like, bead embellishments can be added to the acorns or strap. Blocking the bracelet lightly can help it hold its shape.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Bracelet
- Use stitch markers to keep your stitches organized and prevent mistakes.
- Count your stitches regularly to maintain your pattern consistently.
- Maintain even tension for a tidy finish.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My acorns look misshaped." – Ensure you're working in the magic ring and counting your stitches to keep the form.
- "The bracelet is too tight." – Adjust the chain length based on your wrist size before finalizing the strap.
- "My leaves don’t look uniform." – Check that you’re following each row accurately and not skipping stitches.

## How to Care for Your Bracelet
Hand wash with cold water and lay flat to dry. This will help maintain its shape and prevent stretching.
