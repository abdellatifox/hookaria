## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! The Sage Garden Granny Square Tote is perfect for beginners. You’ll learn how to create granny squares, change colors, and join pieces together, all while making something useful and beautiful. Plus, the repetitive nature of granny squares makes them a great way to practice your stitches.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- dc – double crochet
- sc – single crochet
- sp – space
- [ ] – work instructions in brackets in the same stitch

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** Approximately 14" × 12" (35.5 × 30.5 cm)
- **Gauge:** 4" (10 cm) = 4 granny squares (each square measuring about 4" × 4" / 10 × 10 cm).
- To check gauge, make a granny square with the selected yarn and hook. If it measures smaller or larger than 4", adjust your hook size.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Part 1: Granny Squares
Make a total of 12 granny squares (3 in each color).

1. **Start a Granny Square:**
   - With Sage Green, ch 5, join with sl st to form a ring.
   - Round 1: Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), [2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] in the ring. Repeat [2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] two more times. Join with sl st to top of beginning ch 3. (12 dc, 3 ch 2 spaces)

2. **Rounds 2 – 4:** 
   - Round 2: Sl st into next 2 dc to reach ch 2 sp. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), [2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] in same ch 2 sp. *In each ch 2 sp of the previous round, repeat [3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc]. Join. (24 dc, 6 ch 2 spaces)
   - Round 3: Sl st to next ch 2 sp. Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), [2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc] in same ch 2 sp. *In each ch 2 sp of the previous round, repeat [3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc]. Join. (36 dc, 9 ch 2 spaces)
   - Round 4: Repeat Round 3 for the last color until you reach the desired size of 4" × 4".

3. **Color Change:** 
   - Change colors every round as follows: 
     - Sage Green - Round 1
     - Lavender - Round 2
     - Sunshine Yellow - Round 3
     - Coral Pink - Round 4

### Part 2: Assembling the Tote
1. **Arrange Squares:** Lay out the 12 granny squares in 4 rows of 3 squares each.
2. **Join Squares:**
   - Using a whip stitch or slip stitch, join the squares along the edges. Start with the top row, then continue through the second, third, and finally the bottom row.
3. **Form the Tote Body:**
   - Fold the joined rectangle wrong sides together (about 12" × 42"). Seam the two long sides together using a slip stitch.

### Part 3: Creating the Handles
1. **Handles:**
   - With Sage Green, ch 40.
   - Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across (39 sc). 
   - Row 2 – 4: Ch 1, turn, sc in each sc across.
   - Repeat for 2 handles.

2. **Attach Handles:**
   - Position the handles approximately 5" (12.5 cm) in from each side of the tote opening. Secure with slip stitches and ensure durability by weaving through stitches.

## Assembly & Finishing
- **Weaving in Ends:** 
  - Use a tapestry needle to weave in all yarn tails.
- **Edging:** 
  - Optionally, work a round of sc around the top edge for a neater finish.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Tote
- Use stitch markers to keep track of corners in your granny squares.
- Count your stitches frequently to maintain consistency in size.
- Keep your tension even to avoid uneven edges.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My squares are uneven." – Check your tension and make sure you are using the same hook size throughout.
- "The colors don’t match up." – Ensure you change colors correctly at the end of each round.
- "Handles feel weak." – Make sure to secure them well with slip stitches and weave the yarn through stitches for added strength.

## How to Care for Your Tote
Hand wash in cold water and lay flat to dry. Avoid wringing out the fabric to keep the shape intact.
