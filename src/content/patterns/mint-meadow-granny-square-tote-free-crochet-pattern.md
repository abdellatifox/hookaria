## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- dc – double crochet
- sp – space
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- RS – right side  

**Special Stitch: Granny Square**
1. Start with a magic ring, or ch 4 and join to form a ring.
2. Always work rounds in clusters of 3 dc, separated by ch 2.

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** 36 × 36 cm (14 × 14 in)
- **Strap Length:** 61 cm (24 in)
- **Gauge:** 13 dc and 7 rows = 10 cm (4 in) in granny square pattern.  
To check your gauge, create a swatch that measures at least 10 cm (4 in). Adjust your hook size if necessary.

## How to Crochet Granny Squares (Step by Step)
The granny squares are crocheted in rounds, starting from a center ring.

### Granny Squares (Make 12)
**Color A - Mint Green**  
1. Round 1: Ch 4, join with sl st to form a ring. (4 ch)
2. Round 2: Ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc into ring, ch 2, *3 dc into ring, ch 2*; repeat from * around; join with sl st to top of ch 3. (12 dc, 4 ch-2 spaces)
3. Round 3: Sl st to first ch-2 space, ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in same space, ch 2, *3 dc in next ch-2 space, ch 2*; repeat from * around; join with sl st to top of ch 3. (24 dc, 8 ch-2 spaces)
4. Round 4: Sl st to first ch-2 space, ch 3 (counts as 1 dc), 2 dc in same space, *3 dc in next ch-2 space, ch 2, 3 dc in same space*; repeat from * around; join with sl st to top of ch 3. (36 dc, 12 ch-2 spaces)
5. Finish off and weave in ends.

### Assembling Granny Squares
Once you have made your squares, it's time to assemble them.
1. Join four squares in a row for the base. 
2. Join squares together using a whip stitch or sl st along the edges, matching stitches closely. Make 3 rows of 4 squares each (3 squares wide, 4 squares tall).
3. After linking all 12 squares, weave in any remaining ends.

### Straps (Make 2)
The straps will be worked in a simple single crochet.
1. Using Color A, ch 7.
2. Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (6 sc)
3. Rows 2–51: Ch 1, turn; sc in each sc across. (6 sc)
4. Finish off, leaving long tails for sewing to the bag.

## Assembly & Finishing
Bringing your tote to life is the final step.
1. Position the squares into a tote shape, ensuring the right side is facing out.
2. Attach the straps to the top corners of the tote bag using whip stitch or sc, ensuring they are secure.
3. Optional: Line the tote with fabric for added durability.
4. Weave in all ends and block lightly to straighten the squares.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Tote
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rounds and joins.
- Count your stitches frequently to avoid mistakes.
- Maintain consistent tension to keep squares uniform in size.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My project is uneven." – Check that you're consistently working the same number of stitches in each square. Even tension will help keep squares uniform.
- "My stitches are too tight." – If the gauge isn’t matching, try using a larger hook size to create looser stitches.
- "The bag is not holding its shape." – Ensure squares are correctly joined, and avoid pulling too tightly while stitching them together.

## How to Care for Your Tote
To keep your tote looking fresh, hand wash it in cold water and lay flat to dry. Avoid pulling too tightly on stitches during use to maintain its shape.
