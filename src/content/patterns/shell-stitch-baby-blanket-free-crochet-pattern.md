This cozy shell stitch baby blanket pattern is perfect for keeping your little one warm and snug. The beautiful shell stitch creates a textured look that enhances both style and comfort. This pattern is enjoyable to make, and you will mostly use the double crochet stitch along with the shell stitch.

This project is suitable for beginners who have a basic understanding of crochet techniques. You’ll learn how to create the lovely shell stitch while making a charming blanket for a baby or a decorative piece for the nursery.

## Why You'll Love This Baby Blanket
- Beautiful texture – the shell stitch gives the blanket an elegant look.
- Quick to crochet – complete it in just a few hours.
- Easy to customize – adapt the colors to suit any nursery theme.
- Practical size – perfect for wrapping and snuggling with your little one.

## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern is friendly for beginners because it primarily uses basic stitches. You’ll get comfortable with the shell stitch as you work through the rows, making it a great way to practice your skills.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- shell – (2 dc, ch 1, 2 dc) all in the same stitch
- sk – skip
- sp – space

## Gauge and Finished Size
- **Finished Size:** Approximately 76 × 91 cm (30 × 36 in)
- **Gauge:** 10 cm (4 in) = 12 shells x 6 rows in shell stitch  
To check gauge, work a small swatch in the shell stitch. Adjust your hook size if it's not matching.

## How to Crochet a Shell Stitch Baby Blanket (Step by Step)
The blanket is worked back and forth in rows, starting with the foundation chain. 

### Foundation and First Row
1. Ch 90.
2. Row 1: In the 4th ch from hook, make a shell (2 dc, ch 1, 2 dc). *Skip next 2 ch, shell in next ch.* Repeat from * across to last 3 ch, skip 2 ch, dc in last ch. (30 shells)

### Rows 2-7
The next rows are where you build on the shell stitch pattern.
3. Row 2: Ch 3 (this counts as first dc), turn. In the first shell, make a shell. *Shell in next shell.* Repeat from * across, ending with dc in top of turning ch. (31 shells)
4. Rows 3–7: Repeat Row 2.

### Color Change to Mint Green (AC)
Now it’s time to add a splash of color!
5. Row 8: Change to Mint Green. Ch 3 (this counts as first dc), turn. In the first shell, make a shell. *Shell in next shell.* Repeat from * across, ending with dc in top of turning ch. (31 shells)
6. Rows 9–14: Repeat Row 8.

### Switch Back to Soft White (MC)
Let's return to the main color for a few more rows.
7. Row 15: Change back to Soft White. Ch 3, turn. Repeat Row 2.
8. Rows 16–21: Repeat Row 2.

### Finishing Rows
Finish off with some solid rows to ensure a clean edge.
9. Row 22: Ch 3, turn. Repeat Row 2.
10. Row 23: Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (Final row of 31 shells)

## How to Finish Your Blanket
- Weave in all ends with the tapestry needle.
- Block if necessary to ensure edges are straight and the size is uniform.
- No additional edging is required since the shell stitch provides a lovely finish.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Baby Blanket
- Use stitch markers to mark your first stitch for better accuracy.
- Count your stitches to avoid any mistakes when working through the rows.
- Maintain even tension throughout for a uniform blanket.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My blanket is uneven." – This might happen if stitches are too tight or loose. Keep your tension consistent.
- "I lost track of how many shells I'm supposed to have." – Use a stitch marker or count as you go to stay organized.
- "The blanket curls at the edges." – This may occur if the stitches are too tight. Loosen your grip on the yarn as you crochet.

## How to Care for Your Blanket
To keep your blanket looking fresh, machine wash on a gentle cycle. Tumble dry on low or lay flat to dry. Avoid bleach to maintain colors.

## FAQ
### How many chains do I need for a larger blanket?
For a larger blanket, add multiples of 3 to the starting chain to maintain the shell pattern.

### Can I use different yarn weights?
Yes, just remember that changing the yarn weight may affect the gauge, so you might need to adjust your hook size accordingly.

### How long does it take to make this blanket?
With focused work, you can finish this blanket in approximately 4 hours.
