## Is This Pattern Good for Beginners?
Yes! This pattern utilizes basic stitches and techniques. You'll learn to create stripes, incorporate color changes, and make scalloped edges, all while enjoying the process.

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sl st – slip stitch
- sc – single crochet
- dc – double crochet
- hdc – half double crochet: yarn over, insert hook into stitch, yarn over again, pull through all three loops on hook.
- sp – space
- inc – increase
- BLO – back loop only

## Size and Gauge
- Finished size: 75 × 100 cm (30 × 40 in)
- Gauge: 14 dc and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in).
To check gauge, work a small swatch in your chosen stitch pattern. If your measurements differ, adjust your hook size accordingly.

## Step 1: Create the Base
The base of the blanket is crocheted in rows. 
- Row 1: Ch 111 (this does not count as a stitch). 
- Row 2: Ch 3 (counts as the first dc), turn. Work 1 dc in the 4th ch from the hook, 1 dc in each ch across. (109 dc)
- Row 3: Ch 3, turn. Work 1 dc in each dc across. 
- Repeat Row 3 for a total of 16 rows for the main stripe color.

## Step 2: Change Colors for Stripes
We’ll add stripes to give our blanket some character. 
- Row 17: Change to your second color. Ch 3, turn. Work 1 dc in each dc across. 
- Repeat Row 18 for 4 more rows. 
- Next, switch back to the first color. Ch 3, turn, work in pattern for 5 rows.
- Continue alternating colors for stripes until the blanket reaches 75 cm in height, finishing with a row of the first color.

## Step 3: Add Scalloped Edges
Now, we’ll create the scalloped edges for a neat finish. 
- Round 1: Ch 1, sc evenly around the entire blanket, placing 3 sc in each corner. Join with sl st to the first sc.
- Round 2: Ch 1, *sc in next 2 sts, 3 sc in the next st (corner), repeat from * around. Join with sl st to the first sc.
- Round 3: Ch 1, *hdc in next 2 sts, scallop (5 dc in next st), repeat from * around, ending with a join.
- Round 4: Ch 1, sl st in each hdc and into each scallop across to finish.

## Finishing Tip: Secure Your Ends
Cut your yarn, leaving a tail. Pull the tail through your last loop and tighten. Weave in all ends securely using a tapestry needle.

## Beginner Tips
- Use stitch markers to mark new rows or color changes.
- Count your stitches carefully to maintain an even width.
- Check your tension often to keep your blanket uniform.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- My blanket is too narrow. – I miscounted the starting chains. – Count your initial chains before beginning each project.
- The stripes look uneven. – I didn’t change colors properly. – Pay close attention during transitions, ensuring you complete color changes correctly.
- My scalloped edges are jagged. – I didn’t place my stitches evenly. – Count stitches as you go and adjust your counts if necessary.

## How to Care for Your Baby Blanket
We recommend hand washing your blanket in cold water and laying it flat to dry. This will help maintain the softness and prevent any damage.

### Can I use a different yarn type?
Yes, feel free to substitute with any soft, lightweight yarn that meets the gauge requirements. Just make sure it's suitable for baby items.
