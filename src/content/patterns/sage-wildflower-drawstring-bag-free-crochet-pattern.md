## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- sc – single crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- sk – skip
- st(s) – stitch(es)
- dc – double crochet
- [*...*] – repeat instruction between asterisks as indicated

## Size and Gauge
- **Finished Size:** 25 × 20 cm (10 × 8 in)
- **Gauge:** 12 sc and 14 rows = 10 cm (4 in)
- To measure gauge, create a 10 cm (4 in) square in single crochet. If the gauge doesn't match, adjust your hook size accordingly.

## How to Crochet a Sage Wildflower Drawstring Bag (Step by Step)

### Bag Body
The bag body is made with single and double crochet rows to create the base.  
1. **Starting Chain:** Ch 50.  
2. **Row 1:** Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (49 sc)  
3. **Row 2:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (49 sc)  
4. **Row 3:** Repeat Row 2. (49 sc)  
5. **Row 4:** Ch 1, turn. *Sc in the first 15 sts, dc in the next 5 sts, sc in the next 15 sts, dc in the next 5 sts, sc in the next 9 sts.* (49 sts total)  
6. **Row 5:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each stitch across. (49 sc)  
7. **Row 6:** Ch 1, turn. *Sc in the first 9 sts, dc in the next 5 sts, sc in the next 15 sts, dc in the next 5 sts, sc in the next 10 sts.* (49 sts total)  
8. **Rows 7–18:** Repeat Rows 5 and 6 for a total of 6 rows of sc followed by 6 rows of the pattern. (You should end on Row 18 with 49 sc)  
9. **Row 19:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across. (49 sc)  

### Finishing the Body
10. **Join Row 19 to Row 1:** Fold the piece in half, aligning Row 19 with Row 1.  
11. Sc through both layers to create the side seam. (49 sc in the seam)  

### Completing the Bag
12. **Top Edge:** Ch 1, sc around the top edge of the bag to create a clean finish. (Around 52 sc)  
13. **Drawstring Channel:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in each st across for the first round. (52 sc)  
14. **Second Round:** Ch 1, turn. Sc in the same st as ch-1, sk 1 st, *sc in next st, sk 1 st* across. (26 sc)  
15. **Third Round:** Ch 1, turn. Repeat Round 2. (26 sc)  

### Assembly & Finishing
Weave in all ends with a tapestry needle.  
Insert the drawstring through the channel created in rounds 13–15.  
Gather the top of the bag by pulling the drawstring to close.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Drawstring Bag
- Use stitch markers to keep track of your rows.
- Count your stitches to prevent accidentally skipping any.
- Pay attention to your tension; even tension will create a nice shape.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My project is uneven." – This can happen from inconsistent tension. Practice keeping an even grip while working.
- "The item is too tight." – Check your gauge. If it's too tight, try using a larger hook next time.
- "I skipped stitches when adding the wildflower details." – Always double-check counts and refer back to the instructions as you go.

## How to Care for Your Drawstring Bag
For best results, hand wash your bag and lay it flat to dry. This will help maintain its shape and details.
