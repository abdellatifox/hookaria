This cozy crochet basket weave blanket pattern features a lovely textured design that is both stylish and functional. It's perfect for snuggling on the couch or using as a decorative throw in your favorite space. You’ll learn how to work the basket weave stitch, which involves alternating front post and back post double crochets for that beautiful texture.

This project is suitable for intermediate crocheters who are comfortable with basic stitches and ready to explore a new technique. You’ll enjoy creating a warm blanket while improving your crochet skills along the way.

- Timeless texture – the basket weave pattern adds dimension that looks impressive.

## Skill Level: Intermediate
You should be familiar with these stitches: 
- ch – chain 
- dc – double crochet 
- bpdc – back post double crochet 
- fpdc – front post double crochet 

## Crochet Abbreviations (US Terms)
- ch – chain
- dc – double crochet
- bpdc – back post double crochet
- fpdc – front post double crochet
- sl st – slip stitch
- sts – stitches

### How to Make a Basket Weave Stitch
1. **Base Row**: Work your foundation row with double crochet stitches.
2. **Alternating Stitches**: Every row will alternate between front post double crochet and back post double crochet to create the texture.

## Size and Gauge
- **Final dimensions**: Approximately 127 × 152 cm (50 × 60 in)
- **Gauge**: 12 dc and 8 rows = 10 cm (4 in)
- **How to measure**: Crochet a small swatch of 12 dc for 8 rows and measure it to ensure accuracy.

## Step-by-Step Instructions
The blanket is worked in rows, starting with a foundation chain.

### Foundation
Ch 108. (This will create the foundation for the blanket.)

### Row 1 (Right Side)
Dc in 4th ch from hook, *dc in the next 3 chs; (bpdc in next 4 chs, fpdc in next 4 chs) repeat from * to end. (Total 108 sts)

### Row 2 (Wrong Side)
Ch 3 (counts as first dc), dc in each dc, *bpdc in each fpdc, fpdc in each bpdc; repeat from * to end. (108 sts)

### Rows 3-60
Repeat Row 2 until blanket measures approximately 152 cm (60 in) in length. Change colors every 12 rows:
- Rows 3-12: Soft Cream
- Rows 13-24: Dusty Blue
- Rows 25-36: Charcoal Gray
- Rows 37-48: Soft Cream
- Rows 49-60: Dusty Blue

## How to Add the Edging
With Charcoal Gray, make a slip knot and attach yarn to any corner. 
Ch 1, sc in same sp, sc evenly around the entire blanket, mitre corners (3 sc in each corner).

## Finishing Up
Use a tapestry needle to weave in all yarn ends securely. 

## Blocking
Use pins to block the blanket, shaping the edges. Leave to lay flat until dry.

## Beginner Tips for a Neat Blanket
- Use stitch markers to track your rows.
- Count your stitches as you go to avoid mistakes.
- Maintain consistent tension for a uniform look.

## Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)
- "My blanket is curling." – This can happen if your tension is too tight. Try loosening your stitches.
- "The texture looks off." – Make sure you’re alternating between the front post and back post stitches accurately.
- "I lost track of my rows." – Use markers to keep track, and try making small notes after each color section.

## How to Care for Your Blanket
Machine wash on a gentle cycle and tumble dry on low. Store flat or on a hanger to maintain shape.
